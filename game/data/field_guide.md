# Cubarium field guide
Draft field notes for the app's species pages, which Wrysk edits freely; size, diet lists and live counts come from the sim, not from this file.
The guide lists species in this file's order. `swatch` is the list colour; `habit_label` names the habit row (default Habit for animals, Grows for plants).

## Lanternjaw
id: lanternjaw
swatch: #9168A1
kind: Animal
kicker: Predator
role: Ambush mesopredator
habit: Long stillness, then a brief strike
habit_label: Hunts

A low violet scorpid that hunts by waiting. Its forked tail arches over its back and holds two cyan lures before its face, dead still, until prey comes within reach of its mantis striking arms. By day the lures copy a flower's near-infrared shine. The glow is costly to keep. It takes small ground animals, bellwings at rest and seedporters caught on the ground. At the edge of another hunter's kill, it takes what it can.

## Veilhound
id: chorister
swatch: #6C55D5
kind: Animal
kicker: Predator
role: Pack hunter
habit: Stalk together, then a long rush
habit_label: Hunts

A long violet runner on six legs, dark along the back and pale at the belly and head, its flanks marked with soft dark rosettes. Its jaw plates part sideways around a cyan muzzle; a stiff tail streams behind. Veilhounds hunt by day in packs that share one target. They stalk, then rush, and pick the prey that is alone, wounded or slow to notice. Nothing in the terrarium hunts them. Between hunts the pack rests at its den.

## Frondgrazer
id: frondgrazer
swatch: #FB8162
kind: Animal
kicker: Grazer
role: Ground browser
habit: Cropping low foliage by day

A low coral grazer under one long saddle shield, marked with wine-dark saddles and carried on six short legs. A folded neck unfolds to press an upturned scoop into the leaves above it, and two eye-like stalks stand at the scoop's corners. Herds work the open ground by day, cropping turf, low crowns and vine leaves. When hunters close in, adults bunch together and hunker into their armour, and calves press in beneath an adult's shield.

## Littershredder
id: littershredder
swatch: #FED24E
kind: Animal
kicker: Scavenger
role: Eyeless detritivore
habit: Litter and carrion, by touch and taste

A long, tapering crawler of overlapping gold plates banded in dark brown, with a blunt plough at the front and two feelers trailing down to the ground. It has no eyes. It works through damp litter and carcasses in the dark, finding food by taste and touch, and climbs rock walls with ease. When a hunter's footsteps reach it, it freezes, then slips away. It lays its eggs in the litter.

## Bellwing
id: bellwing
swatch: #C19BFC
kind: Animal
kicker: Nectar feeder
role: Flower visitor
habit: Sipping nectar from bloomcrown and latticevine

A small flyer shaped like a hanging plum bell, its open rim bright magenta, held up by four lilac vanes edged in cyan. A fine peach siphon, coiled at the tip, reaches into flowers for nectar. Flowers here shine in near-infrared under the red sun, a colour the bellwing sees and we cannot. It learns which beacons give nothing, the lanternjaw's lure among them. It flies by day, from first light into dusk, and heavy rain grounds it.

## Loftstrider
id: loftstrider
swatch: #22B4D2
kind: Animal
kicker: Browser
role: Canopy browser
habit: Reaching into the canopy with its fronds

The terrarium's tallest animal, built in sixes around one upright axis. An onion-shaped body, teal below and violet above, stands on six long legs with pink knees, and six magenta eyes look out all around it. A long neck lifts a pink head with the mouth on top, ringed by pale tusks and frilled fronds that reach into the canopy for leaves. It faces a lone hunter but flees a pack. Without vaulttree groves it may not last.

## Capgnawer
id: capgnawer
swatch: #A76982
kind: Animal
kicker: Fungus eater
role: Fungivore
habit: Glowcaps on fallen wood, after dark

A small, rounded rose-mauve animal under a two-lobed mantle, its bark-like back flecked with pale spots. Six pale mandibles frame a narrow mouth, flanked by light pink sensor patches. In the dark it climbs over fallen logs, finding glowcaps by scent and by their faint cyan light, and gnaws the caps and the threads beneath them. Where the fungus runs out it makes do on litter. It lays its eggs beside a fruiting glowcap.

## Ripple-snail
id: ripple-snail
swatch: #5081F2
kind: Animal
kicker: Grazer
role: Shoreline film grazer
habit: Scraping film from wet rock

A small snail under a low azure dome with shallow growth ridges, a pale mantle edge and a dark foot. Two pink-tipped palps reach from the open front to scrape the glassy film off wet rock in the shallows and along the shore. In the dark and in rain it roams further ashore, then returns to the water before it dries out. It lays its eggs on damp banks just above the waterline. Threatened, it pulls into its shell.

## Seedporter
id: seedporter
swatch: #889335
kind: Animal
kicker: Fruit eater
role: Grove glider
habit: Fruit in the canopy, by day

A moss-green climber with a pale belly, a dark back stripe, large dark eyes and a coiling tail that grips like a hand. Skin membranes stretch from its big arms to its hind legs, and it glides on them between trees. It climbs stems and vine-covered walls, sits upright to eat and crosses open ground on all fours. It eats lanternberries and latticevine beads and spreads their seeds. It snatches bellwings, and escapes hunters into the trees.

## Bloomcrown
id: bloomcrown
kind: Plant
kicker: Flower
role: Sun-loving flower
habit: On bright, open, well-drained ground

A slim violet stem holds up a ring of hot-pink vanes around a pale, glowing central pod, with periwinkle straps at its base. In bloom, the pod's tip turns warm orange. Bloomcrowns stand on ridges and terraces in full sun, tolerate dry spells and die where water pools. Their flowers carry nectar, and bellwings come to sip it. Grazers crop the foliage they can reach.

## Umbrellafrond
id: umbrellafrond
kind: Plant
kicker: Frond
role: Shade-maker of wet hollows
habit: In damp low ground, in sun or shade

A violet stem raises three neat tiers of lacy teal fronds, each frond ribbed in pale cyan, so the crown reads almost as geometry. Umbrellafronds grow where water gathers, rooting deep in wet hollows and managing on little light. Their tiers cast a deep shade, and the mossy velvetpad beneath them thins out. They spread by spores and by runners, so a hollow often fills with a stand of them.

## Springturf
id: springturf
kind: Plant
kicker: Grass
role: Wild grass of open hills
habit: In sweeping stands on sunny, drained ground

A low, spreading clump of ribbon leaves, deep teal at the base and bright mint at the tips. Springturf is the terrarium's wild grass. It covers open, sunny slopes in sweeping stands, comes up through the velvetpad moss and slowly replaces it. It is the grazers' main crop and regrows fast from its base. In a drought it dies back and waits, then greens again when water returns. Its seeds ride the wind and hitch on passing animals.

## Stonecushion
id: stonecushion
kind: Plant
kicker: Cushion plant
role: Bare-rock pioneer
habit: On rock ledges, rooted in a soil pocket

A compact mound of plump lilac lobes, pricked with tiny cyan pores, sitting on bare rock. Stonecushions live where little else can, on rock lips and ledges with their roots reaching into a pocket of soil. They need very little water and grow very slowly. Standing water drowns them. On a cliff they share the rock with latticevine: the cushions keep the lips, and the vine climbs the faces.

## Velvetpad
id: velvetpad
kind: Plant
kicker: Moss
role: Mossy early colonizer
habit: First on bare ground; stays in shade and damp

A soft violet carpet with a raised purple rim, tufted with cobalt moss and threaded with thin cyan channels. Its spores reach new ground first, lit or shaded, and carpet it quickly. Out in the open, springturf comes up through the moss and takes over. Under ledges and crowns, in shade and damp, the velvetpad stays. It is more cover than pasture, though grazers do crop it.

## Glowcap
id: glowcap
kind: Fungus
kicker: Decomposer
role: Wood fungus
habit: On fallen wood and litter, in shade and caves

A broad, tilted purple cap with dark spots and a vivid cyan brim, rising on a violet stalk from fallen wood. Cyan threads of mycelium glow through the log it lives in. Glowcaps are not plants: they live on dead wood and litter, in caves, shade and damp corners, and open sun dries them out. Capgnawers seek out their caps. When a glowcap dies, its body returns to the litter.

## Vaulttree
id: vaulttree
kind: Plant
kicker: Tree
role: Canopy builder
habit: Slowly, from a bright gap in deep soil

An uneven trunk of violet bark plates opens into arching limbs that hold dark-teal shelves of foliage, each rimmed in mint, while fine cyan tendrils hang far down the trunk. A vaulttree starts only in a bright gap with deep, airy soil, grows slowly and lives long. Seedporters roost in it and loftstriders browse its shelves. When it dies it falls, leaving a long line of dead wood and a new gap of light.

## Lanternberry
id: lanternberry
kind: Plant
kicker: Shrub
role: Fruiting shrub
habit: At grove edges and in bright, moist gaps

A fan of violet stems with small blue leaflets, hung at the tips with closed deep-plum lanterns seamed in magenta. As the fruit ripens, the lanterns glow warm orange. Lanternberries grow at grove edges and in bright, moist gaps. Seedporters eat the fruit and carry the seeds away to sprout elsewhere. Fruit left uneaten falls to the ground, and the shrub rests before it fruits again.

## Siphonreed
id: siphonreed
kind: Plant
kicker: Reed
role: Shallow-water reed
habit: Standing in sunny shallows

Slim celadon stems rise from the water, swelling near the top into broad, rounded pink fan heads. Siphonreeds stand in sunny shallow water, and spread along the shallows by runners and by seeds the water carries. They grow in narrow clumps, a line of pink heads above the surface.

## Latticevine
id: latticevine
kind: Plant
kicker: Vine
role: Rock-face cover
habit: Up rock walls and down from ledges

A dense cover of blue rosettes on plum runners, pinned to the rock by small holdfasts. It roots in soil at the foot of a wall or on a ledge, then climbs up the face or hangs down from above, spreading as far as the rock and water allow. It flowers in patches, never all at once: pale lilac bells with glowing cyan mouths feed bellwings, and magenta bead clusters feed seedporters, who climb the vine to reach them.
