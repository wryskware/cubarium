**Independent ecological review: meadows, consumers, and persistence**

My recommendation is to make the landscape a mosaic of different recruitment opportunities, and to make consumers respond to *accessible production*. Grass needs places where woody seedlings cannot reliably become canopy. Small animals need a short, affordable path from food to offspring. Hunters need to find prey, eat what they kill, and leave enough reproductive prey somewhere to replenish their hunting grounds. None of these outcomes follows merely from having the appropriate species on the roster.

This is exploration, not an accepted design. I inspected the working tree at HEAD `6f8610f4`, using simulation source and presets, without reading existing design documents or following their references in code comments. The five observations in the request are supplied evidence; I did not reproduce those runs or inspect their calibration. Defaults and presets already contain several relevant mechanisms, so their presence below is not a claim that the observed runs exercised them successfully. No simulations were run for this review. Literature supports the ecological reasoning; the proposed Cubarium mechanisms and experimental verdicts are my own inferences.

**1. Give grass a place where being short is an advantage.**

The present rules explain why improved grass growth alone is unlikely to secure meadows. Springturf is a ground cover; lanternberry and vaulttree can recruit through ground covers. When recruitment wins, `germinate` removes the incumbent stand and records an `Overtopped` death immediately. This is a separate route of loss from subsequent shading. A woody seedling need not first survive beside the grass it replaces. See [the recruitment lottery and replacement, seeds.rs:466–655](../../crates/cubarium-voxel-flora/src/seeds.rs#L466).

Shade itself is consequential: foliage stock, footprint and porosity determine attenuation, and species differ in their response to the remaining light. Springturf's `light_half` is 1.0, lanternberry's 0.4, and vaulttree's 0.6. The establishment rules distinguish terrain sky visibility from a further canopy profitability check. Passing that check is only a newborn's reference-condition budget test, not a guarantee of survival through a whole day or a dry spell. See [light response, step.rs:1187–1215](../../crates/cubarium-voxel-flora/src/step.rs#L1187), [canopy profitability, step.rs:3861–3922](../../crates/cubarium-voxel-flora/src/step.rs#L3861), and [habitat gates, step.rs:4141–4192](../../crates/cubarium-voxel-flora/src/step.rs#L4141).

I would build the meadow niche from two interacting mechanisms, then assess whether a third is needed.

**A. Let soil capacity and water history limit woody size and establishment.** A shallow, exposed, intermittently dry soil should support a small perennial grass more readily than a large woody crown. A deeper, reliably supplied but aerated soil should permit shrubs and trees. The distinction should arise from rooting opportunity, water demand and survival through lean periods, rather than an elevation label or a predetermined meadow mask.

The code already supplies a starting point: grass roots search depth 2/radius 1, shrubs 3/1, and trees 6/2; grass has drought curing and reserve-funded recovery. However, the root-box function simply includes available soil voxels and the moisture reading averages their available water. A deeper configured search does not itself require a minimum deep-soil volume. Establishment checks moisture and saturation, not a species-specific anchoring or soil-capacity requirement. Thus “give trees deeper roots” can enlarge their access without imposing the tradeoff that reserves shallow soils for grass. [Root boxes and moisture, step.rs:1895–1943 and 1977–1992](../../crates/cubarium-voxel-flora/src/step.rs#L1895); [grass traits, lib.rs:2073–2176](../../crates/cubarium-voxel-flora/src/lib.rs#L2073); [tree traits, lib.rs:2556–2633](../../crates/cubarium-voxel-flora/src/lib.rs#L2556); [shrub traits, lib.rs:2662–2729](../../crates/cubarium-voxel-flora/src/lib.rs#L2662).

My proposed addition is a size-dependent requirement for usable rooting volume or sustained water supply. A woody seedling may germinate after rain, but its attainable size and chance of surviving the next dry interval depend on its site. A grass survives that interval by retaining a small crown and reserves. Trees must still be able to mature and reproduce on suitable ground. Apply this to ongoing growth as well as admission: a one-time gate is easily bypassed by an unusually wet establishment moment.

Do not assume that the driest ground automatically becomes meadow. Grass currently needs available water of at least 0.3 to establish, versus 0.2 for vaulttree, although adult grass tolerates lower water. That distinction may be reasonable for a seedling, but it means dry adult survival and dry-site recolonization are different questions. Likewise, curing does not provide indefinite storage: the desktop preset's maximum cured interval is about 1.83 world days. The relevant niche is recurring wet establishment opportunities followed by survivable dryness. [Desktop grass overrides, terrarium.toml:247–260](../../config/desktop/terrarium.toml#L247).

This is a proposed resource tradeoff for this terrarium, not a claim that rooting depth universally explains real savannas. Higgins, Bond and Trollope showed why demographic bottlenecks and disturbance can matter more than simple root separation. Their fire model motivates the importance of the seedling-to-adult transition; it does not establish that Cubarium needs fire. [Higgins et al. (2000), *Journal of Ecology*](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1046/j.1365-2745.2000.00435.x).

**B. Let an established sward resist seedlings, and let browsers maintain some openings.** A mature tree can shade grass strongly while grass can suppress a young tree. This asymmetry is the missing reciprocal interaction I would investigate first. Woody invasion into dense turf should depend on the turf's actual condition, competition and seedling survival. A repeated fixed recruitment chance ultimately gives woody seeds many opportunities to erase the incumbent without enduring that contest.

The most faithful small extension would retain the grass while a separately accounted woody recruit passes through a vulnerable establishment stage. Light, water and browsing then decide whether it escapes above the sward. If that representation is too expensive, an establishment-stage survival calculation could approximate the contest, but it should debit the seed and reflect living turf condition; merely lowering `overtop_per_check` delays succession without demonstrating coexistence.

Frondgrazers could supply a useful role here by taking reachable woody shoots as well as grass. Springturf already has protected basal foliage (`graze_refuge = 0.4`), while seedlings and adult woody crowns have different profiles and heights. Feeding is already restricted to the foliage layers the mouth reaches. These are promising ingredients for a grass that tolerates repeated cropping better than a woody recruit tolerates repeated loss of its growing shoot. I would first measure whether the current anatomy and budgets already create this difference. [Grass refuge, lib.rs:2164–2173](../../crates/cubarium-voxel-flora/src/lib.rs#L2164); [feeding by reachable layer, fauna step.rs:2531–2643](../../crates/cubarium-voxel-fauna/src/step.rs#L2531).

There is a crucial qualification: eating grass alone can *help trees*. A Serengeti experiment found that grazing released seedlings from grass competition when browsing and fire were absent. Therefore I would not equate “more grazers” with “more meadow.” The required role is demonstrable suppression of woody recruitment together with grass recovery. [Donaldson et al. (2022), experiment and associated data](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.jq2bvq8bc). Experiments also show that early browsing can be a strong seedling filter, with survival depending on species and seedling age. [Archibald et al. (2021), *Journal of Ecology*](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1365-2745.13745).

The grazer benefits by finding food, not by knowing the desired landscape. If present frondgrazers consistently crop turf and overlook profitable woody shoots, change their food selection or add a browsing role only after demonstrating that gap. Do not increase grazing pressure indiscriminately. Dense ungrazed grass may resist seedlings through competition; frequently browsed patches may resist them through herbivory. Both can contribute to the mosaic.

**C. Use gap renewal only if the first two mechanisms leave too little open habitat.** Tree death can reopen light, but a short-lived grass patch between successive woody occupants is not necessarily a persistent meadow. Nor will faster seed dispersal save grass if no landing site permits adult replacement. The desktop grass seed bank has a maximum age of 52,696 seconds—about 7.32 world days—not anything close to the supplied 150-day settling time. A late gap needs nearby reproductive grass or another surviving propagule source. [Grass seed-bank settings, terrarium.toml:257–258](../../config/desktop/terrarium.toml#L257).

First measure natural canopy turnover and grass recolonization. If productive ground closes indefinitely, a localized, resource-driven disturbance role could help: a large browser damaging crowded woody patches, for example. It would need a benefit from visiting those patches and a limit to its destructive effect. I would defer adding it until the simpler recruitment mechanisms are assessed. Periodic clearing merely to restore a cover percentage would conceal the reason meadow recruitment fails.

The intended spatial outcome is therefore:

| Site condition and local history | Expected opportunity, to be tested |
| --- | --- |
| Open, shallow or intermittently dry soil with surviving grass crowns | Meadow cores; woody recruitment usually fails before canopy escape. |
| Moderately moist, aerated soil with intermediate light and successful shrub recruitment | Lanternberry groves; local seed rain and suitable neighbouring sites give aggregation. |
| Sufficient rooting volume and water supply, with bright recruitment gaps or browsing escape | Vaulttrees establish, mature and seed new suitable sites. Summits qualify only when their resources qualify. |
| Saturated margins | Wetland plants and moss retain their distinct opportunities. |
| Grove edges, browsed paths and canopy gaps | Moving boundaries and occasional woody recruitment, rather than permanent exclusion of trees everywhere grass grows. |

I would judge this by recovery from rarity, replacement of adults, and connected meadow area. A few surviving grass stands or seed packages are insufficient. Stable coexistence concerns the ability to recover at low density, not just slow disappearance; this is the useful test from coexistence theory. [Chesson (2000), *Annual Review of Ecology and Systematics*](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev.ecolsys.31.1.343).

**2. Make the small consumers respond on the timescale of their food.**

The three “bugs” should not receive one undifferentiated reproduction multiplier. Littershredders consume litter and carrion; capgnawers consume fungal caps; ripple-snails consume film. Their abundance should follow the production and accessibility of those different foods. Strictly, the snail's film pathway is not detritivory, which matters when diagnosing why a litter pulse does not help it. [Diet rules, body.rs:1004–1578](../../crates/cubarium-voxel-fauna/src/body.rs#L1004).

For littershredders, the supplied gut bottleneck is credible mechanistically. Stomach capacity is `stomach_k × body`; digestion removes approximately `stomach_stock / digestion_s` per second, adjusted by juvenile throughput. Assimilation is further limited by food yield, food mineral content and reserve room. Growth spends reserve; reproduction requires size, reserve, eligibility history and an affordable egg package. Eggs also need a suitable, uncrowded laying site. A shorter birth interval cannot bypass any of these. [Gut rules, body.rs:874–953](../../crates/cubarium-voxel-fauna/src/body.rs#L874); [digestion, step.rs:822–876](../../crates/cubarium-voxel-fauna/src/step.rs#L822); [growth, step.rs:959–993](../../crates/cubarium-voxel-fauna/src/step.rs#L959); [eligibility, step.rs:2820–2844](../../crates/cubarium-voxel-fauna/src/step.rs#L2820); [laying, step.rs:3133–3278](../../crates/cubarium-voxel-fauna/src/step.rs#L3133).

My preferred life history is a small, feeding juvenile that reaches reproductive size promptly and thereafter directs sustained surplus into repeated affordable clutches. Bigger food patches should support more successful recruits, with crowding or food depletion eventually lowering recruitment. That describes a numerical response to abundance; it does not require continually enlarging every adult. The current presets already set juvenile gut mass exponent 0.75, brood allocation 0.8, and a possible 1,800-second wait for a fuller clutch. Those are mechanisms to audit, not additions to claim as new. In particular, a long clutch wait can reduce response speed even when eventual clutch size looks good. [Desktop littershredder settings, terrarium.toml:314–348](../../config/desktop/terrarium.toml#L314).

Once access, allocation and laying behaviour are working, changing a throughput rate may genuinely be necessary. “Roles before rates” should not forbid fixing an actual physiological bottleneck. The preliminary budget is:

`maximum assimilated income ≲ effective yield × stomach capacity × throughput factor / digestion time`.

Subtract maintenance, movement and growth before estimating the time needed to fund eggs. This is an upper bound, not a predicted generation time: incomplete feeding, mineral limitation, incubation and the reproduction gates make the realized interval longer. Check every term at hatchling, breeding and adult size.

For capgnawers and snails, first distinguish low food production from failure to reach food. An empty gut beside a rich but unreachable resource is a movement or feeding problem. An empty gut on exhausted accessible patches is a production problem. Faster reproduction cannot supply food that does not exist. Capgnawers already have a fungus-proximity laying requirement; inspect whether suitable caps remain near hatchlings when they emerge. Film consumers similarly need persistent wet feeding surfaces, not merely a wet world average. [Capgnawer preset, terrarium.toml:53–86](../../config/desktop/terrarium.toml#L53).

The useful speed target is ecological: offspring must begin replenishing a productive patch before depletion or predation removes its breeding population. Against the supplied two-hour prey decline, a shorter laboratory generation that still takes many hours would not by itself solve the mismatch. Measure successful recruits per unit of food production as well as time to first clutch.

**3. Treat lanternjaw performance as an encounter-and-consumption problem before a kill-rate problem.**

The small-map and full-map observations are not contradictory. They may describe high encounter rates in a confined prey patch and low encounter rates across a fragmented terrarium. A stock-and-flow model can constrain sustainable consumption, but a mean prey stock cannot establish what a moving hunter actually encounters.

The current patrol selects patches from *prey food*, searches locally with sight and tremor, remembers fruitless patches, and gives up on prolonged travel. Its patch ranking uses horizontal positions; candidate selection does not itself establish a traversable route or matching vertical surface. This makes productive but inaccessible ledges, or food-rich patches without prey, plausible false destinations. These are hypotheses to trace, not demonstrated causes of the reported starvation. [Patch selection, hunt.rs:284–397](../../crates/cubarium-voxel-fauna/src/hunt.rs#L284); [local pursuit and patrol, hunt.rs:442–614](../../crates/cubarium-voxel-fauna/src/hunt.rs#L442).

I would let successful encounters update where the hunter searches, retain exploration, and make reachability part of destination choice. Prefer repeated visits to productive accessible hunting areas over walking toward the strongest food signal irrespective of route. Measure the complete chain: reachable prey → detected prey → pursued prey → strike → kill → swallowed carrion → assimilated income. Improving strike power addresses only one link.

There is a particularly concrete consumption concern. A kill deposits a corpse on the prey's face; it does not directly fill the hunter's gut. Feeding later requires a positive feed action and mouth contact. The patrol's waiting and travelling branches return `feed = 0`, and its prey scan loses a target when that animal dies. I found no explicit corpse-eating branch in that patrol function. Thus an animal may successfully kill and then fail to exploit the resulting food. Other controller or timing paths could still allow meals, so a one-kill trace should settle this before attributing full-world starvation to it. [Kill deposition, step.rs:2172–2245](../../crates/cubarium-voxel-fauna/src/step.rs#L2172); [strike versus feeding, step.rs:1619–1662](../../crates/cubarium-voxel-fauna/src/step.rs#L1619).

The behavioural remedy is an explicit sequence: pursue, kill, locate the carcass, eat until satiated or the accessible meal is exhausted, then resume hunting. It both rewards active hunting and prevents additional kills made while a useful meal lies uneaten. It should use the real mouth, gut and carcass stock, not a free post-kill energy award.

Efficient hunting alone could worsen the small-map prey decline. My complementary proposal is productive prey refuge: small crevices, climbable surfaces, wet margins or litter interiors that small prey can use and reproduce in, with movement back into hunting areas. The existing difference between climbing littershredders and non-climbing lanternjaws offers one basis; measure whether it functions at the animals' actual dimensions. Protection must preserve production and allow exchange, rather than permanently sealing every prey animal away.

If several hunters still repeatedly exhaust the same patch, test local interference or spacing, with reproduction depending on realized food surplus. Three hunters should not necessarily extract three independent shares from one small feeding ground. This is secondary to fixing meals and prey recruitment, and needs its own test rather than an arbitrary global population ceiling.

I would not lead with species loyalty or simply enable the existing switching knob. The current default has `share_switching = false` and `attack_explore = 1`; an optional encounter-share formula exists, but its strongest preference can remain one even when all prey are scarce. Relative abundance is not absolute prey replenishment. Refuges, satiation and profitable patch departure can protect rare prey while retaining opportunistic attacks. [Attack preference, hunt.rs:139–210](../../crates/cubarium-voxel-fauna/src/hunt.rs#L139); [defaults, knobs.rs:593–615](../../crates/cubarium-voxel-fauna/src/knobs.rs#L593).

Also assess each prey species separately. Making abundant shredders support more hunters can increase pressure on a scarce capgnawer or snail. Shared predators can link prey negatively even when those prey do not compete for food—the apparent-competition mechanism formalized by Holt. This is a reason to test cross-species effects, not a prediction that a larger shredder population must be harmful. [Holt (1977), *Theoretical Population Biology*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0040580977900429).

**4. Give grazing a bottom-up limit as well as Veilhound predation.**

I would retain the Veilhound's grazer-regulating role. The concern is making it the only protection against foliage collapse. A temporary loss of one pack should produce more grazing and less recruitment, but surviving plant refuges should permit recovery. Otherwise a small predator fluctuation can erase the productive base before the predator returns.

Several bottom-up checks already exist: bites diminish with local stock, gut capacity limits intake, inaccessible crown layers and grazing floors protect foliage, and offspring cost reserve. The first question is whether these stop grazer recruitment *early enough*. A large accessible standing crop can finance a birth pulse although its regrowth cannot support the resulting juveniles. Current eligibility reads size and reserve history; it is not a direct forecast of future forage production. [Stock-dependent bites, step.rs:796–810](../../crates/cubarium-voxel-fauna/src/step.rs#L796); [reproduction eligibility, step.rs:2820–2844](../../crates/cubarium-voxel-fauna/src/step.rs#L2820).

If the current checks respond too late, I would make breeding depend more strongly on sustained net intake and condition across a foraging cycle, and let depleted patches induce departure. Use accessible intake after travel costs, not total world foliage. Keep basal recovery and woody shoot browsing in the same experiment: protecting every sapling from grazing would defeat the meadow mechanism, while indiscriminate stripping would defeat the entire food web.

The goal is bounded fluctuations and vegetation recovery during a predator absence, not identical grazer abundance with and without predators. Predator fear might redistribute grazing, but I would not assume it creates a stabilizing landscape: the Donaldson experiment above found no detectable effect of lion risk on the herbivore-mediated seedling outcome.

**5. Close carrion use through behaviour, and distinguish caps from fungal biomass.**

Carrion already has three potential consumers in this roster: littershredders, lanternjaws and Veilhounds. The ground pool also decomposes to respiration and soil mineral. Therefore “no consumer” and “no outlet” are both inaccurate diagnoses. The question is why consumption is weak relative to deposition. [Diets, body.rs:1088, 1237 and 1528](../../crates/cubarium-voxel-fauna/src/body.rs#L1088); [decomposition, flora step.rs:3704–3756](../../crates/cubarium-voxel-flora/src/step.rs#L3704).

Distinguish a high stock from continuing accumulation. With approximately constant deposition `J` and first-order decay `kC`, even unconsumed carrion can approach `C = J/k`. That would be bounded storage but a weak scavenging role. Measure deposition, consumption and decomposition independently before describing it as runaway growth.

First fix carcass retention and feeding for successful hunters, then give the existing detritivore an effective response to carcass patches: locate them, feed there and recruit nearby when surplus permits. Its local meal selector currently compares available stock, not expected assimilated return per unit of feeding time; abundant litter can therefore win a choice even if carrion would be the better meal under different yields or digestion times. Test that before introducing a new scavenger. [Meal choice, body.rs:2649–2673](../../crates/cubarium-voxel-fauna/src/body.rs#L2649). Facultative scavenging is a major real energy pathway, and discovery and handling capabilities affect its realization. [DeVault, Rhodes and Shivik (2003), *Oikos*](https://nsojournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1034/j.1600-0706.2003.12378.x).

If the remaining carrion is on surfaces no existing consumer can exploit, then a mobile carcass specialist is a defensible missing role. Give it a finite food-funded brood response, and test whether its subsidy to predators harms other small prey. Simply raising decay may bound the pool but bypasses the visible consumer response Wrysk wants.

For glowcap, coexistence with plants is already represented spatially: saprotrophs occupy a separate `FaceLayer::Fungus`, and germination contests run separately for the two layers. Preserve that. It removes direct exclusion for a plant slot; it does not remove shared-resource or shading effects. [Face layers, lib.rs:803–817](../../crates/cubarium-voxel-flora/src/lib.rs#L803); [layer-specific germination, seeds.rs:488–523](../../crates/cubarium-voxel-flora/src/seeds.rs#L488).

Capgnawers remove cap tissue. Glowcap spore production already follows cap fullness, so grazing can reduce spread without directly removing its structural mycelium. That is useful, but “caps cropped” does not prove that mineral immobilization in the fungus has been relieved. Track caps, structural stock, mineral held and substrate turnover separately. If excess lies in long-lived mycelium, the answering process is mycelial turnover or a consumer that actually eats that tissue; more cap mouths may miss the relevant stock. Real fungus–grazer interactions can stimulate or suppress fungal activity depending on density and identity, which cautions against assuming all grazing reduces fungal biomass. [Cap-linked propagation, seeds.rs:815–827](../../crates/cubarium-voxel-flora/src/seeds.rs#L815); [Crowther, Boddy and Jones (2012), *The ISME Journal*](https://academic.oup.com/ismej/article/6/11/1992/7587746).

A further structural detail deserves an explicit budget: glowcap has a nonzero dark-seep subsidy. Its growth code can import organic matter, energy and mineral in dark habitat, beyond the material withdrawn from dead wood and litter; darkness can also permit establishment without the normal substrate minimum. This is an implemented external input, not evidence that the ledger is broken. It does mean the fungus is not wholly limited by recycled wood. If the intention is a wood-funded decomposer, make that subsidy draw from a finite supplied resource; if the subsidy is intentional, retain it but measure its ecosystem-wide rate and the eventual mineral outlet. Adding consumers cannot remove an ongoing mineral input from a materially closed system—they redistribute it. [Glowcap defaults, lib.rs:2451–2486](../../crates/cubarium-voxel-flora/src/lib.rs#L2451); [seep accounting, step.rs:3154–3171](../../crates/cubarium-voxel-flora/src/step.rs#L3154); [establishment exception, step.rs:4180–4186](../../crates/cubarium-voxel-flora/src/step.rs#L4180).

More generally, detritus connects food supply, nutrient return and predators. A shrinking litter pile is not sufficient evidence of improvement if its mineral merely moves into another inaccessible compartment. This is why I would measure flows and biological response together. [Moore et al. (2004), *Ecology Letters*](https://rosemondlab.ecology.uga.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/Moore-et-al.-2004-EcolLet.pdf).

**6. First checks and results that would change my mind.**

These are proposed experiments, not completed validations. Start with the smallest mechanism check, then compare matched branches across seeds. Existing long-run observations justify testing slow succession; they do not justify starting every change with another long run.

| Idea and stock needing a response | First check | Evidence it works | Evidence against it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Soil/water niche answers grass loss and woody closure | At equal light, compare grass and woody juveniles across usable soil volume and wet–dry sequences. Read full-day budgets and adult attainable size. | Grass replaces itself on some sites where woody recruits fail; trees and shrubs mature and reproduce elsewhere. | All species fail in meadow sites; woody exclusion requires unrealistically absolute drought; or changed rates only postpone closure. |
| Turf competition and browsing answer excessive woody recruitment | Follow a funded woody seed through dense turf, clipped turf and bare ground, with and without shoot browsing. Account for the incumbent grass throughout. | Dense turf or repeated shoot loss lowers canopy escape; release permits some woody recruits; grass recovers after cropping. | Grass disappears on seed arrival regardless of later recruit fate; browsing mainly removes grass and accelerates woody spread; no tree recruits survive anywhere. |
| Gap renewal answers long canopy occupation | In a mature branch, identify actual new light gaps and the nearest viable grass propagules. Test a localized disturbance role only if natural turnover is inadequate. | Grass reaches gaps and becomes reproductive; meadow area renews while groves and tree recruitment continue. | Gaps are immediately woody again, grass banks are already dead, or the disturbance suppresses all woody reproduction. |
| Faster food-funded bug life cycles answer litter accumulation and slow prey replacement | Compare rich accessible food, normal accessible food, and food made hard to reach. Track hatchling-to-first-young time, stomach, reserve, brood, mineral limitation and every laying refusal. | Extra food raises successful recruitment promptly; recruitment falls as that food is depleted; ordinary patches sustain replacement. | Birth timers shorten but young do not appear; adults are gut-limited regardless of food; or faster cohorts exhaust their own resource and disappear. |
| Reachable hunting patches and carcass meals answer full-world hunter starvation | Trace one hunt through a meal, then compare navigable and obstructed layouts at equal prey supply. Verify the actual controller used. | More accessible prey produces more assimilated income, with most usable kills followed by feeding. | More kills leave the gut empty; destinations are repeatedly unreachable; increased travel costs exceed the added meals. |
| Prey refuges and, if needed, hunter spacing answer local overkill | Compare one and three hunters on matched hunting areas, with productive refuges and exchange switched on/off; repeat on the full terrarium. | Every prey lineage retains recruiting sources; hunter food budgets close; depleted patches recover and are recolonized. | Only permanent isolation saves prey, rare lineages vanish behind stable total prey counts, or persistence comes solely from hunter starvation. |
| Sustained-condition breeding and patch departure answer grazer overshoot | Branch a vegetated world with the Veilhound present and absent. Track accessible regrowth, intake, births and protected foliage, not just total foliage. | Predation changes abundance, but grazer recruitment also slows as net forage falls; plants survive and recover. | Foliage still collapses before recruitment responds, or grazers can persist only while the predator continually removes their young. |
| Scavenging response answers carrion accumulation | Deposit equivalent carcasses at reachable and inaccessible sites with existing consumers. Compare arrival, ingestion, offspring and decay separately. | Carrion attracts consumers and supports recruits; the pool returns toward its prior range after the pulse. | Decay alone explains clearance, consumers never find it, or carrion-supported predators drive another prey lineage extinct. |
| Cap grazing, mycelial turnover and a bounded subsidy answer fungal accumulation/mineral sequestration | Cross capgnawer access with wood supply and dark subsidy; follow caps, mycelium, spores, mineral and plants sharing the site. | Caps support consumers, fungal spread responds, structural mineral turns over, and plants persist alongside it. | Caps disappear but mycelium/mineral continue accumulating; plants decline; or ongoing external mineral input has no corresponding outlet. |

For whole-web evaluation, retain the distinctions in that table. Compare fluxes per accessible habitat area and per consumer, not just counts per map. The first short segment must show movement, eating, operation of the mechanism under test and conservation after loading. Follow the working policy's staged checkpoints and re-evaluation; use small maps for plant mechanisms and a larger spatial setting for predator conclusions. No new long-run automated test is needed for this report.

Then branch mature worlds for the slow questions: adult replacement, meadow area, seed-bank renewal, dead-wood turnover and external nutrient accumulation. A two-hour success cannot establish 150-day coexistence. Use multiple seeds and continue only promising arms through enough of the relevant slow clock to distinguish renewal from surviving founders. Across weeks, record each lineage's viable reproductive stages and extinction events, connected meadow/grove area, replacement of trees, and the trends in litter, wood, carrion and mineral compartments. A population held only by a compute ceiling is not ecologically bounded.

For the final robustness check, start both grass-rich and woody-rich landscapes, and temporarily reduce each major consumer in turn. Success means that suitable habitats retain their roles and the web returns to a range of abundances after disturbances, allowing local turnover. It does not require identical mosaics or constant population counts. In a finite stochastic terrarium, absolute persistence of every species forever cannot be inferred from a finite run; the practical objective is repeated replacement and a low observed extinction risk over the requested weeks, without periodic reseeding disguising failure.

I would begin with the one-kill-to-meal trace, the stage-by-stage bug budget, and the woody-seed-in-turf experiment. Each can expose a structural obstruction quickly. Those findings should determine which new behaviour is actually needed before a broad rate-tuning pass.
