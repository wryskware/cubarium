# Vinecoil endpoint retile: independent temporal review

**Decision: approve for bounded integration**, under the constraints below. This is
evidence, not a production switch. Reviewed Astra's committed study **6a5521e** and
its follow-up **5e5ffd7** ([disposition](astra-vine-wind-study-2026-09-13.md),
`art/studies/vine-wind/` README, `evidence.json`, `renderer.patch`, `main.rs`), the frozen worktree
`captures/build-cache/vine-wind-frozen` at 9cf0e1d with only that patch applied, and
the native 256×128 PNG frames under `captures/vine-wind-retile-2026-09-13/`. I did
not play the optional MP4s or the viewer; every visual claim here is from still
frames, so nothing below asserts smooth playback or physical-panel appearance.

## Visual findings

The plant survives. In every neighbourhood the mint crown, the magenta/teal helix
with its magenta nodes, and the anchored root read identically in both variants;
the difference image is confined to the helix and crown edge, with no new shape,
no detached or repainted row at the retiled endpoint, and no seam artefact where
the interior and Right crowns cross onto Top. At the interior peak the retiled
column visibly leans further as one curve; at 12× it is obvious, at native 64 px it
is a modest but real change in the stem's set. Vertex and Right motion stays faint
in both, as the wind field intends.

Halo, blur and new detail: exactly one pixel per full track is ever lit in the
retiled variant and never in the original, at peak luma 0.07/255 (a bilinear support
tail, invisible). No pixel is lit only in the original. Peak pixel luma is
identical. The retiled helix is fractionally softer at peak because a larger
sub-pixel displacement spreads the same bilinear footprint; this is the same
softening the 0.9 px bare spiretree already accepted, not a new blur.

Brightness/coverage: interior mean 8-bit luma sum rises 2.2% and the mean count of
pixels above 1% luma rises from 306.7 to 311.8. That is displacement spreading, not
compensation, and it is not visible in the stills.

## Independent temporal observations (stills-derived)

| Interior full, 1,200 frames | Original | Retiled |
|---|---:|---:|
| Largest single-pixel change between adjacent frames | 2/255 | 2/255 |
| Mean adjacent-frame RGB L1 (8-bit units, whole net) | 0.998 | 1.043 |
| Largest excess of one boundary over its two neighbours | 0.94 | 0.92 |
| Luma reversals where both steps ≥ 2/255 | 0 | 0 |
| Per-row tip lean at frame 340, top rows (px) | −0.39..−0.41 | −0.72..−0.79 |

Vertex and Right full tracks: largest single-pixel adjacent change 2/255 in both;
tip lean at frame 317 roughly doubles (0.05 → 0.08 px vertex; Right within 0.01 px).
Growth tracks: both variants share the same single large first-frame step and,
on the two edge fixtures only, one seam-corner pixel jumping 160–195/255 at frame
1177→1178, identical in old and new (`(64,60)` vertex, `(124,0)` Right). That is a
pre-existing cap-glide/seam matter at the diagnostic fixtures, not caused by this
retile and outside its scope; root may want to look at it separately.

Frames where the two variants are byte-identical: interior 0–33 and 1114–1199
(120 total), vertex/Right 112 in the tail plus the head. That matches the packet's
0.6 s travel delay; every differing frame has nonzero amplitude. Nothing in the
adjacent-frame data distinguishes the retiled track from the original except that
more pixels move further; every reversal in either track is a single 8-bit step,
consistent with quantised sub-pixel motion under the 2.3 s flutter rather than any
endpoint or vertex pop.

## Mechanics check

- The endpoint is the identical vine clip's image rows 4..7 stamped at tile 10, whose
  local heights 8..12 are global 40..44, the rows old tile 9 painted as its rows
  12..16 (image rows 0..3). Four-row periodicity makes the content the same; the
  cleared row 0 was only ever painted there. Bend base 32 plus local 8..12 gives the
  same global `H`, so `D(H)` is unchanged.
- `Mask::Axial { reveal: grown − 32 }` fades the endpoint's row centred at 11.5 at the
  same `grown` the old `Strip` faded tile 9's row at 15.5, so growth is not re-enveloped.
  The study's exact quiet sweep (heights in eighths through 9) covers this.
- `stamp_pose_in_chart` uses the production displacement and coverage expressions
  and unfolds from tile 9's anchor with query ≤ 13 px while rejecting samples beyond
  9 px of the tile 10 centre. The retained-chart regression (Top(0,62)) shows why the
  owner must stay tile 9.
- Not hardened: `heading.normalized().unwrap()`, the endpoint parked in `vine.cap`
  (so any custom pack giving the vine a crown would be misread as an endpoint), the
  opt-in keyed on `trunk_strip(vine)`, and a public `draw_column`.

## Constraints for integration

1. Derive and cache separately named vine trunk/endpoint clips at pack load;
   never borrow `cap`. For the intact-atlas integration, validate exact four-row
   periodicity on the original frames **before** clearing any rows. Then derive
   the clear-ended trunk and rows4..7 endpoint with the exact original clock.
   Missing opt-in metadata retains old strips bit for bit; malformed explicit
   opt-ins are errors. Root corrected this wording: requiring an already-cleared
   row0 to equal its painted periodic counterpart would reject the intended art.
2. Keep the endpoint's bend budget in the column minimum at base 32, as the study's
   `tall_bend_budget` cap slot already does.
3. Keep the query superset bounded (owner-to-centre distance + 9 ≤ 32) and the
   painted footprint at 9; keep the physical-neighbourhood test.
4. Replace the heading unwrap with the production fallback; keep `draw_column`
   private or study-gated.
5. No wind, phase, flutter, or budget retune and no timers; the gain comes only
   from the vine's admitted room (0.42 → 0.90 desired tip on a vined spire).
   Glasscane stays at its own 0.47.
6. Re-render a copied actual world with typical/high column populations and check
   cadence before any switch; my review measured no render cost.

## On the follow-up commit

5e5ffd7 changes no capture, patch or renderer line, so the frame evidence above is
unaffected. It corrects one supplemental assertion that had passed
`bend_headroom` its arguments in the wrong order and was therefore vacuous; I had
not caught that, and the 4,320 larger-query comparisons it sat beside construct
`Bend` by named field and stand on their own. Root's intact-atlas representation
(keep the original periodic trunk bytes, add an explicit vine-strips selector,
derive and cache the cleared trunk and endpoint at load, reject rather than fall
back on a malformed opt-in) satisfies constraint 1 more cleanly than a clear-ended
atlas and I prefer it. Its budget note is the one that matters: an opted-in vine's
budget must be the minimum over the derived trunk at tile 9 and endpoint at tile
10 and must replace the raw trunk's 0.465, or the retile changes nothing.

## Limits

Still frames only; no viewer, MP4 or panel viewing. I did not rerun the study's
tests, bakes or timings, and did not check the reported test counts. The visual
benefit at native 64 px is modest and my approval rests on the change being
free of temporal artefacts in these frames and on the plant reading unchanged,
not on the enlarged sheets. Whether 0.8 px of lean is worth a renderer facility is
Wrysk's call on the cube.
