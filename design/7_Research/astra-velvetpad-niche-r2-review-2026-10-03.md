5. **Important — Six hours demonstrates slow refill, not its endpoint; drained shade has a structural access problem.** Evidence: proposal `:84–97,121–138,162–175`. Six hours is three world days, enough to reject the specified six-hour target. Seed 3's 2,152 → 3,592 → 5,305 drained occupants at 2/4/6 h show ongoing expansion, not a plateau. Conversely, no amount of waiting makes a seed pass an impossible terrain-light gate. The grid identifies stonecushion as a potentially viable low-light adult with slow juvenile growth, while the other tested competitors require substantially more light. Fresh C1 worlds also start with fewer plants (for example seed 3: 164 versus 215), so their low occupancy is assembly under new gates, not simply recently vacated mats. The four-hour retreat continuation still has 70% of the original drained velvetpad sites occupied by velvetpad. **Change:** remove the conclusion that established mats retreat “over about 3 h”; that is a constant-zero-income probe timescale, contradicted as a world-level summary by intermittent rewetting. Diagnose bare sites by missing seeds, failed establishment, juvenile death and slow growth. For the slow clocks, continue the existing paired saves for a bounded 1–2 h and inspect slopes; do not restart another universal six-hour check or promise eventual shaded refill. Keep terrain shade and canopy shade separate when choosing a remedy.

6. **Important — The producer and detrital losses create food-web risks that occupancy cannot clear.** Evidence: proposal `:163–170,183–186`; `crates/cubarium/examples/velvet_niche.rs:510–539,600–608`. These are plants-only runs. Fewer low mats can reduce accessible grazing production even if taller plants add comparable mass; more glowcaps cannot substitute for leaves. Less litter can constrain shredders and saprotrophs and alter mineral return, with downstream effects on consumers and predators. Existing litter, dead wood, animal reserves and a temporary dieback pulse can delay these effects. Conversely, low litter stock could partly reflect faster consumption, so its reduction alone does not establish a proportional production loss or starvation. **Change:** complete the already-scoped paired fauna measure from `design/handoffs/velvetpad-study-2026-10-03.md:59–62`, using established baseline/C1 plants as well as observing the transition. Report edible foliage by reachable layer, fixation, litter input/decomposition/consumption, dead-wood trends, intake by food type, B, R-hat, access, sated share, reproduction and body/reserve trends. Present these as consequences for the owner's choice, not an invented prohibition on trying C1 on the development display. Faster dieback is especially poorly motivated while replacement production is missing: it removes food sooner without supplying a competitor.

7. **Important — Waterlogging tolerance and flood survival are separate; the observed drowning sink is plausible, not evidence that saturation tolerance should be undone.** Evidence: proposal `:144–170`; `crates/cubarium/examples/velvet_niche.rs:262–264,543–565`. C1 permits rooting in saturated soil but retains a 0.1 m standing-water limit. A bank can pass establishment and flood later; the held-water grid does not exercise that sequence. Its result is therefore compatible with flood losses. The wet populations still recruit strongly in the measured worlds. **Change:** first obtain event-based flood depth, duration and lost organic mass, split by seedling/adult and repeated recolonization at the same sites. Accept some marginal-site mortality if the refuges and food budget remain productive. If recurrent small floods destroy a material share of production, propose a modest, measured flood-tolerance or establishment-margin change separately; do not make velvetpad arbitrarily submersible or restore the saturation exclusion that C1 needs. Any faster water-stress mortality must be checked against D1 again, because drought endurance and rapid drought eviction pull in opposite directions.

8. **Important — Choose a replacement strategy before adding mortality; the four options are not substitutes.** Evidence: proposal `:121–134,179–184`, and its resolved light gates and growth thresholds at `:61–67,84–97`. **(a)** accepts a transition time but does not fix uninhabitable drained shade. **(b)** target-scoped overtopping can replace an occupied pad where a viable competitor actually arrives; water-stress dieback only creates a vacancy. Today's unrestricted `overtop_per_check` would also affect other ground covers, so a global positive value is not the proposed scoped treatment. **(c)** allowing drained shade to remain velvetpad's broadens the niche; merely adding a shade establishment ceiling to C1 would shrink it further and would not restore dry-ground income. Restoring that income needs an explicit adult water/light tradeoff, and ordinary canopy shade could then give velvetpad a large refuge again. **(d)** can fill the physiological/access gap but should target a particular competitor rather than make every plant shade tolerant. **Change:** compare a small competitor-specific adjustment, guided by the newborn grid, with a separately scoped overtopping arm. Stonecushion is a reasonable candidate to investigate because its adult shade budget already works; lowering its establishment gate alone is not proof that its slow juveniles will recruit. Require successful competitor recruitment and wet-niche persistence in both vacant and occupied-site fixtures before a broad run.

Present **(c)** as the explicit alternative if Wrysk prefers a lush shaded floor to a strictly wet-ground niche; it changes the ecological goal and requires an adult tradeoff, not just a light gate. Correct the totals and removal accounting, then use the bounded continuation and paired fauna measure to show the consequences of that choice.
