# Astra — community assembly review, 2026-10-03

Reviewed `design/community-assembly-2026-10-03.md` and main at `948682f7`, including the post-repair-3 measure and current search, introduction and census checkpoint code. This is advice for Wrysk, not a decision. No simulations were launched. Earlier reviews' warnings about inaccessible food, initial endowments and unsupported predator recruitment still apply; their historical parameter values do not override the landed gut.

Diet-based arrival and established-world training are sound directions. The proposed supply calculation is not yet a live readiness estimator or an equilibrium solver. Adopt those directions after correcting resource allocation, predator accounting and the treatment of lineages that never arrive.

1. **Important — Question 1: readiness is implementable, but not by substituting live counts into the current screen.** **Evidence:** proposal:27–38; `crates/cubarium-search/src/es/voxel/live/screen.rs:668–836`; `crates/cubarium-voxel-sim/src/habitat.rs:1084–1202,1214–1272`. The sim exposes useful ingredients: stand structure and edible layers, ground pools, vine faces and phenological stages, fauna bodies, route components and actual food withdrawals. `lineage_food` already maps reachable stocks for walkers. Its `production_per_h`, however, is a configured rate times current structure/foliage, not measured replenishment after resource limitations. The search screen is more optimistic still: maximum plant wood, estimated founder counts, assumed light/moisture and whole-floor film production. Nectar/fruit acceptance uses nearby sources and configured rates; flight capability or a source in a fan is not proof the controller can reach and feed there. The repair-3 measure reports bellwings reaching almost no flowers in five of eight worlds (`design/metabolism-meals-2026-10-02.md:343–354`).

   **Change:** implement readiness as a rolling, spatially indexed estimate of **realized edible production plus a separately reported edible stock buffer**. Use source-level production/withdrawal/loss accounting; net stock change alone confounds production with consumption, decay and phenology. Add counters where the exposed state lacks those flows. Apply actual mouth geometry, mineral-limited yield and habitat connectivity. Keep model estimates labelled as estimates, and verify access with short arrival observations. Reuse units and geometry helpers across screens/readiness/founding, but keep optimistic parameter bounds distinct from live evidence. A held threshold is persistence filtering; if readiness can turn off again, specify separate on/off thresholds rather than calling a timer alone hysteresis.

2. **Blocking — Questions 1 and 3: the same food cannot support each lineage independently.** **Evidence:** proposal:30–37,85–94; `crates/cubarium-search/src/es/voxel/live/screen.rs:734–821`; `crates/cubarium-search/src/es/voxel/live/balance.rs:16–33,71–73`. Screen food sums are computed separately for each eater. Applying them as arrival budgets or `K_L` duplicates shared foliage, litter, fruit and prey. Setting every lineage to half its independently calculated K is not a community allocation: three consumers each assigned half the same supply already demand 1.5 times it. Sequential arrivals also need to reserve the supply committed to incumbents and earlier admissions.

   Demand `N×f×body` is awake resting **assimilated** matter per ecological day. Raw plant production cannot be compared directly with it. Sleep reduces basal spending, while travel, growth, reproduction, provisioning and parasites add costs. Margin must not conceal all those conversions.

   **Change:** use one local shared-resource budget, allocating each food source once across resident and prospective consumers. Convert each allocation at the eater's effective yield, use rhythm-aware basal demand plus explicit activity/family allowances, and retain a buffer for seasonal gaps and uncertain access. Define actual body/age composition, not only adult mass. Treat `K_L` as a provisional food-budget upper bound under a stated allocation, not carrying capacity. Bound total bodies and placement attempts so low-upkeep candidates cannot create enormous populations and consume the search budget merely through the initializer.

3. **Blocking — Question 2: prey production is the better flow, but 3–10 is not sound in the proposed raw units.** **Evidence:** proposal:39–44,90; `crates/cubarium-search/src/es/voxel/live/screen.rs:607–618,708–726,823–833`; `crates/cubarium-voxel-fauna/src/step.rs:833–864,2674,2813–2823,2989`. The current screen's carrion estimate treats every possible newborn as an eventual full adult and adds a lifespan carcass flow. It is an optimistic readout, not observed harvestable prey production. The new per-lineage growth ledger helps, but reproductive investment is a transfer into offspring/eggs, not independent new food. Adding whole offspring packages to later growth can count the same transferred reserve twice. Founder reserves can also fund an early growth/birth pulse without renewable replacement.

   Let P be renewable, eligible prey production in **raw organic units/day**, D predator demand in **assimilated units/day**, y effective carrion yield, e the fraction actually captured and eaten before loss, and β the allocated harvest share. The budget is `β × P × y × e ≥ D`. At y=0.5 and e=1, β=0.1–0.3 requires **P/D ≥ 6.67–20**, not 3–10. Even in assimilable units, exactly 30% corresponds to 3.33, not 3. A 10–30% share is a proposed tuning range here, not an established Cubarium bound.

   **Change:** expose harvest share explicitly and separate it from yield, scavenger competition and capture/decay losses. Measure birth structural biomass and subsequent structural growth without counting reserve transfers twice; distinguish gross production, deaths and the portion available to predators. Require a nondepleting prey cohort and sufficient **standing eligible prey** as well as sustained production. Filter by actual prey windows, participating pack strength and reachable components; reserve one shared harvest allocation across predator lineages. Add a separate host-availability and host-budget check for the lanternjaw bank: prey production alone does not ensure hatchling hosts or successful rider growth.

4. **Important — Question 3: computed starts replace default bias with model bias; one hour does not remove either.** **Evidence:** proposal:79–94; `crates/cubarium-search/src/es/voxel/live/screen.rs:738–789`; `design/metabolism-meals-2026-10-02.md:343–375`. Productivity has units of mass/time, not plant density. Inferring a mature grove from it also needs light, space, water, mineral supply, substrate, phenology and age-dependent mortality. Plants-only maturation is useful but does not establish a grazed community. Candidate-dependent founding additionally gives a candidate more injected bodies and stored matter when its own approximate capacity formula says it deserves them; that is a moving test fixture and can reward errors in the initializer.

   One hour is **half a world day**. It cannot wash out even the approximately 1.3-day chorister interval or 1.19-day rider development, much less 6–16-day lifespans. A temporary funded birth pulse can survive that relaxation.

   **Change:** begin with a small, shared ensemble of richer, explicitly constructed starting states at several resource/population levels, paired across candidates. Include both actually grown states and constructed states when practical. Book all injected organic matter, mineral and energy, including mature wood, soil/film stocks, animal reserves, stomachs and eggs. Specify valid age/size/reproductive states rather than independently drawing incompatible ages and sizes. Report startup separately and use interval trends in stores, production and recruitment to judge relaxation; keep one hour only as an initial observation window. Candidate-specific setups may become a useful second treatment, but should not be the sole ecology-search comparison or be called predicted equilibria.

5. **Important — Question 4: evaluate assembly and established persistence as separate objectives.** **Evidence:** proposal:70–99,118–120; `config/training/search/roster.toml:104–116`; `crates/cubarium-search/src/es/voxel/live/targets.rs:401–456`. Established starts answer Wrysk's immediate concern: a predator should get an established prey community, and a high browser should get reachable grown vegetation. They do not show that those communities assemble naturally. Conversely, an assembly-only early horizon can spend every candidate's budget waiting for trees, while revealing little about later predator behavior.

   **Change:** use established-state evaluations for the broad ecology grid and fixed-ecology brain comparisons, with paired initial states and explicit initial-resource accounting. Run a smaller staged-assembly arm and require finalists to show both assembly and post-arrival persistence. Report the two results separately; do not blend an assembly failure into a harmless average. Full bare-world assembly need not run for every genome. Today's founding remains useful as a disclosed early-community diagnostic until richer starts exist, but cannot rank full-community predator or loftstrider sustainability. Decide explicitly when those lineages move from readout-only to scored; the current flags will otherwise keep established-world failures unpenalized.

6. **Blocking — Question 5: never arriving must not become the best search strategy.** **Evidence:** proposal:64–66,72–77,121–122; `crates/cubarium-search/src/es/voxel/live/targets.rs:310–312,441–455`. `lost()` requires `founded > 0`; balance penalties also require founding, and unfounded lineages escape replenishment penalties. These rules are sensible for a deliberately partial roster but unsafe if the candidate's own readiness gate decides whether a required lineage is ever evaluated. Waiting until “every lineage has arrived” also never terminates when one dependency cannot establish. Saving only successful grown worlds would discard the hard seeds and create survivorship bias.

   **Change:** track a fixed required roster with explicit states: pending, ready-but-unplaceable, arrived, recruited/established, extinct, and deadline missed. Keep positive graded progress from readiness deficits and access/recruitment stages, but give a required lineage missing at `max_wait_s` an explicit assembly failure. Record the reason: inadequate production, contested supply, missing habitat/den/host, failed placement, or failure after arrival. Do not force immigration to erase it. A late arrival starts its own post-arrival observation requirement; a newly buried egg bank is not an established predator. Fix the seed panel before testing and report failures/censored waits rather than silently replacing those seeds.

7. **Important — Arrival APIs provide building blocks, not the proposed placement behavior.** **Evidence:** proposal:43–57,62; `crates/cubarium-voxel-sim/src/habitat.rs:719–736,1427–1475,1521–1585,1721–1764,1789–1818`. Existing code distinguishes **bodies, packs and clutches**. `introduce_packs(n)` requests packs, not n adults; `bury_founding_clutches` expands clutches to eggs and returns eggs buried. `introduce_lanternjaws` places free bodies and must not substitute for the proposed bank arrival. Pack placement requires a dry, fitting den in a prey component. These methods do not optimize the place with the most renewable supply or necessarily place the requested count.

   **Change:** specify arrival units and expected eventual demand for each founding mode; place through the existing commands so introduced stocks and controllers are installed correctly. Add a general staged body-arrival wrapper and preserve actual counts/failed attempts. Choose a viable local component with the resource budget, then choose a valid arrival face inside it. “Nearest edge or wall” is a presentation preference, not a habitat guarantee; wrapped seams are not ecological outside edges. Validate route/headroom/water and a usable food patch at the actual site. Report ready-but-unplaceable separately from nutritional failure, and commit resource reservations only for arrivals actually placed.

8. **Important — Grown-world reuse needs an episode contract beyond three saved layers.** **Evidence:** proposal:72–77; `crates/cubarium/examples/voxel_census.rs:654–726`. The census does save world, flora, fauna and minute, and restores layer state. On load it refuses preset/config/seed changes, reinstalls heuristics and then requested policies, and reconstructs `Sim` with `SimConfig::default()`. Controllers' internal memory is not saved. This supports fixed-ecology brain evaluation, but is not already a search API for changing the candidate's ecology in a saved world. A schema match establishes load compatibility, not identical biology or parameter values across builds.

   **Change:** define a reusable episode initializer with matched layer files, saved tick/phase, resolved parameters, build and policy identities, and a stated controller reset/burn-in rule applied equally to all candidates. Use checkpoint-relative ledger baselines; existing cumulative assimilation and births must not enter a new candidate's score. Persist the new arrival scheduler's pending roster, sustained-readiness history, deadlines and immigration state alongside the layers. Keep checkpoints disposable under the repository policy; regenerate when relevant code/config changes, not only on schema bumps, and do not make regrowth of an entire library mandatory for unrelated builds. Measure actual readiness times before promising 1–3 days: the ongoing 16-hour plant soak spans eight world days and is not yet evidence of a particular maturation deadline.

9. **Important — B and R̂ are better diagnostics than a two-hour alive count, but neither proves spare carrying capacity.** **Evidence:** `crates/cubarium-search/src/es/voxel/live/balance.rs:16–33,137–180`; `config/training/search/roster.toml:104–116`; `design/metabolism-meals-2026-10-02.md:356–375`. B divides assimilation by upkeep, motor and host rider losses; growth, reproduction and provisioning are intentionally reported separately. R̂ extrapolates assimilation minus upkeep/motor per adult-day across lifespan and offspring cost; it does not subtract rider losses, separate juvenile growth income, measure recruitment survival or include observed predation mortality. Access and sated share can be NaN when there are no relevant body-ticks. They are useful explanations, not direct measurements of spare prey production or long-term replacement. Current targets also still retain stage/replenishment terms; changing count terms to readouts does not mean only the new measures are scored.

   **Change:** retain B/R̂/access/satiety alongside cohort size, actual births and maturation, cause-specific deaths, stores and producer stocks over time. Use introduction-adjusted, phase-aligned windows and enough post-arrival exposure to interpret them. Do not map absent/undefined measures to success. Before using R̂ for predator admission, create an explicitly harvestable-production estimate rather than reusing the fecundity proxy. Preserve the earlier review's distinction between a funded first birth and recruitment sustained by new food. Diagnose the unexplained empty feast readout and timeout-heavy chase world before attributing all remaining predator trouble to a small prey base; richer starts must not conceal a control/access defect.


## Revision 2 check

Verdict: **accept with changes**.

Reviewed proposal revision `dc69f7e5`; its text is unchanged in the current checkout (`9d8a9daa`). Statuses below concern the proposal, not completed implementation. The shared E0/E1 initializer can proceed first with the restrictions below. Original findings remain unchanged.

1. **A1 — resolved.** R3 replaces parameter-rate substitution with spatial, realized production/withdrawal/loss accounting and a separate stock buffer; R4 adds actual placement checks and two readiness thresholds (proposal:178–190,206–208,222–226). **Change needed:** no further design change; implement the stated counters and verify actual diet access rather than promoting the optimistic screen into a measured guarantee.
2. **A2 — resolved.** Sources are allocated once, effective yield converts raw allocations to assimilated demand, family/activity costs are explicit, K is only a food-budget bound, and initialization is capped (proposal:180–194). This resolves the original blocking double-allocation and demand-unit errors. **Change needed:** maintain those allocations as arrivals succeed or fail; the early E1 recipe must remain fixed across candidates as R2 promises, rather than being resized by the candidate's resource budget.
3. **A3 — resolved.** R5 uses `β P y e ≥ D`, structural births plus later structural growth, a shared predator allocation, standing eligible prey and host checks (proposal:235–254). The arithmetic is correct: at y=0.5 and e=1, β=0.3 requires P/D≥6.6667 and β=0.1 requires P/D≥20. **Change needed:** implement P as renewable production demonstrated over a window, not merely positive structural growth paid from founder reserves; R5's nondepletion condition and R6's new-food requirement already call for that distinction. Do not reuse the old optimistic adult-equivalent birth estimate.
4. **A4 — resolved.** Fixed, paired constructed/grown states replace candidate-specific inferred equilibria; injections are booked, ages/sizes are consistent, and relaxation is judged from trends (proposal:145–176). **Change needed:** none to this choice. Treat “roughly 3×” and “one pack sized to the prey” as recipe values selected once per shared start, not per candidate. E1 is a rich test setup, not proof of equilibrium.
5. **A5 — resolved.** Established and assembly evaluations are distinct, finalists must pass both, and full-community scoring removes the three readout-only exemptions in the established setting (proposal:131–143). **Change needed:** retain E0's diagnostic status and verify the effective scoring flags by setting; do not impose established-community pass criteria on the E0 control.
6. **A6 — partial (important).** The fixed required roster, explicit states, failure reasons, no forced rescue and post-arrival windows remove the original *never-arrives* escape (proposal:198–213). However, the only explicit deadline is for **arrival**, not establishment. A lineage can arrive just before its deadline, never recruit, and remain indefinitely awaiting a pass; a bank can remain eggs indefinitely. **Change needed:** bound the post-arrival window and define failure if required establishment is not demonstrated by its end, including extinction or persistent eggs/riders without the required recruitment. Stop the episode at a bounded total horizon and retain that failure. A required arrival/establishment failure must contribute to assembly fitness even when the ordinary lineage statistics are absent.
7. **A7 — resolved.** Founding modes, actual placement counts, viable-component placement and resource commitment after placement are now explicit (proposal:215–228). **Change needed:** implementation should distinguish the clutch-count input from the egg-count return of `bury_founding_clutches`, and packs from adult bodies when calculating demand. These are now compatible with the stated design, not reasons to delay the initializer.
8. **A8 — partial (important).** The initializer contract now covers checkpoint identity, controller reset/burn-in, relative ledger baselines, relevant regeneration and saved scheduler state (proposal:162–173,230–231). Applying candidate parameters over loaded state is new and underspecified; see N1. The old 1–3-day spin-up estimate at proposal:77 also remains unsupported unless explicitly withdrawn. **Change needed:** specify the parameter/state contract and label spin-up duration as measured/unknown until readiness data exist. Fixed burn-in is not proof that ecological startup bias has vanished.
9. **A9 — resolved.** R6 retains demographic and stock evidence, rejects undefined measures as success, excludes R̂ from predator admission and distinguishes funded first births from recruitment; R5 names the control-defect dependency (proposal:255–266). **Change needed:** retain these distinctions in acceptance and reports. A named repair package is not evidence that the feast/chase defects have actually been fixed; verify its resulting behavior before interpreting predator balance.

### New findings

10. **N1 — Important: define what “apply the candidate's parameters over the loaded state” preserves.** **Evidence:** proposal:162–168; `crates/cubarium-voxel-sim/src/params.rs:158–179`; `crates/cubarium/examples/voxel_census.rs:678–726`. Parameter resolution validates configuration; it is not a state migration. Changing stomach k, body scale, reserve capacity, tissue composition, plant capacities or lifespan against an existing body can leave over-capacity stores, incompatible geometry, or already assigned lifespans inconsistent with the candidate. Rescaling stocks to match the new settings would instead give each candidate different injected matter, defeating a literal shared-state comparison.

    **Change:** make the first grid's allowed override set explicit. Rate-only parameters that leave stored state valid can be applied while preserving absolute stocks and age/state; hold state-coupled traits fixed until their semantics are specified. For the broader search, choose and document conservative handling of existing over-capacity stores, geometry and assigned lifespan/reproduction state; reject incompatible states rather than silently deleting or creating matter. Run derived-parameter resolution and install the resulting configuration into every affected layer. Record baseline-start parameters separately from candidate parameters. This is part of step 1, not deferred readiness work.

11. **N2 — Important: the build order is right at the front but not at the E2 dependency.** **Evidence:** proposal:158–160,180,233–256,268–274. The initializer and fixed E0/E1 recipes are useful without a finished admission estimator. But step 3 claims to make E2 before step 4 supplies the predator admission budget. A full-roster staged run cannot safely admit its predators without that budget, and cannot finish a required-roster E2 by leaving them pending forever. R3's statement that construction uses its shared budget also conflicts with demanding the complete realized-budget machinery for step-1 E1 construction.

    **Change:** build (1) the initializer, compatibility rules, fixed E0/E1 recipes and paired scoring; (2) counters/shared budget; (3) scheduler infrastructure and nonpredator admission; (4) predator harvest/host admission after the control fixes; then generate full E2 and enable full assembly acceptance. Alternatively build predator admission before completing step 3. State that early E1 is a prescribed, conservatively booked fixture, not a candidate-sized setup requiring R3 to exist. Do not delay the E0/E1 grid for E2, and do not call an intermediate predator-free run complete assembly.

12. **N3 — Important: failed E2 seeds and adaptive relaxation need explicit evaluation outcomes.** **Evidence:** proposal:136–141,158–160,166–173. R1 requires all nine lineages in an established start; R2 also keeps seeds where some never arrive. Both are sensible, but such a failed-growth checkpoint is not an established start, and a failure under the library-building policy is not automatically a failure of every candidate later compared. Likewise, “judge relaxation by trends” must not let each candidate choose an arbitrarily late scoring window and discard its collapse.

    **Change:** keep failed seeds in the assembly/provenance panel with their failure recorded, without silently substituting another seed or scoring absent lineages as established success. Define whether candidates get a bounded opportunity to complete those starts in the assembly arm; report that separately from E1/successful-E2 persistence. Prespecify phase-aligned scoring windows and a maximum startup allowance for paired comparisons. Record non-relaxation and failures during burn-in instead of excluding them; retain startup trends as evidence, with complete ledger baselines at each window boundary.
