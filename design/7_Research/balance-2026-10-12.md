# BALANCE: bracketing Wrysk's topic-8 rates

Package 9 of `design/behaviour-ai-2026-10-11.md` (§6). Evidence and a working model, not a
decision. The target (topic 8, "(a) makes sense"), per world-year of 10 h: about one
loftstrider, mostly strayed calves; an adult once in several years; a standoff every 1–2 h,
usually ending with the pack giving up; a hound killed by kicks about once in a few years.

## The rate model

Per world-year (`Y` = 10 h), for each encounter type `k` with live rate `E_k` (encounters an
hour) and per-encounter outcome shares `P(· | k)` from the harness:

- loftstrider kills a year = `Y · Σ_k E_k · P(kill | k)`;
- adults killed a year = `Y · Σ_k E_k · P(adult killed | k)`;
- standoffs an hour = `Σ_k E_k · P(gave up | k)` over the grouped types;
- hound deaths a year = `Y · Σ_k E_k · P(hound death | k)`.

Encounter types and where their numbers come from:

| type | `P(· | k)` from | `E_k` from |
|---|---|---|
| guarded family (2 adults + calf vs 2–4) | harness `family-*` | wide A/B (pending) |
| grouped adults, no calf | harness `adults-*` | wide A/B |
| strayed calf | harness `strays-3` (young-roam scene: excursions at 120/h, pack 40 m out), per excursion | excursions per calf-hour (knob) × share of excursions with the pack in range (wide A/B) |
| lone calf | harness `calf-*` | wide A/B (orphans, dispersal) |
| lone, lame or old adult | not staged yet | wide A/B |
| resting body | not staged (the harness is a daylight, awake scene) | wide A/B (deaths by posture) |

The harness ends an episode at the first give-up, so `P(· | k)` is per standoff. A pack that
re-tests the same family after its rest (`rest_s`, `shun_s`) shows up in `E_k`, not in `P`.

## What the model needs (budget before tuning)

At the design's 5–10 standoffs a year (`E_family + E_adults` ≈ 0.5–1 an hour):

- adults: 0.2–0.33 a year ⇒ `P(adult killed | standoff)` ≈ **2–6 %**;
- hound deaths: 0.2–0.33 a year ⇒ `P(hound death | standoff)` ≈ **2–6 %**;
- calves: ≈ 0.7 a year from strays ⇒ excursions a year with the pack in range ×
  `P(calf killed | excursion)` ≈ 0.7.

## Step 1: the two open bugs

**`faces()` and the `facing 0` trace.** Traced `family-3` seed 1 every 0.1 s with each adult's
held defence intent (now printed by `engage_trace`). `facing` read 0 exactly while both adults
had their backs to the pack: they held `move_off` (a threat inside `threat_m`, beyond
`guard_m`, no calf within `guard_m` of it), walking away at 120–171° off the nearest hound,
and then turned in place into `guard`. The pack read the guard stance (×`guard_factor`, risk
doubled) and gave up at 4.1 s with the adults still turning. Over six more traces
(`family-4`, `adults-4`, `adults-2`; seeds 2–3; 2,368 assessments) the traced count matched
the bodies' true headings except in 200 rows, all within 0.4 s of a turn and at the 45° edge:
the hound's 4 Hz remote sight (`perception.remote_hz`; a track's heading is the body's at the
last sighting). Not a mid-turn read and not the wrong body, so `faces()` is unchanged; the law
test `a_body_that_turns_to_face_the_hound_reads_facing_from_its_next_sighting` pins it.

What the trace does show: a guarded family's adults spend the first seconds of a standoff
facing away (`move_off`), and the pack's give-up comes from the stance, not from facing.
That is LS-DEFEND's design (§3.1) and not changed here.
