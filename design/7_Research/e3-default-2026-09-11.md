## Reading

- Reproductive opportunity is large: median time to first reproduction about
  5 minutes, median parent age at birth about 8 minutes, ancestry depth
  rising to 117–127 in a day (roughly five generations per hour). The
  ecology's time-scale table should say minutes per generation, not the
  10–90 minute "lineage turnover" row.
- Each completed life yields almost exactly one offspring (0.995), with a
  Gini of 0.73: most organisms die childless at a median age of 12 minutes,
  all from starvation, while a minority reproduce repeatedly. Nothing reaches
  the two-hour age limit.
- Seventy-one of seventy-two founder lineages vanish within the first cycle
  (median last descendant at about 19 minutes); the entire day descends from
  one founder in every seed. With identical genomes this is neutral drift
  through the population troughs (15–30 organisms), so the effective
  population is far smaller than the census. For M3 this means mutation-borne
  variation will coalesce quickly unless troughs are shallower or lineages
  are more local; E4/E5 must report lineage survival alongside trait
  distributions, and the 72-founder diversity should not be assumed to
  persist.
- Births per hour alone would have suggested a healthy, diverse population;
  the lineage record says otherwise, which is exactly why E3 exists.
