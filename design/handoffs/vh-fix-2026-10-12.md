# VH-FIX: lone calves are catchable, the choice follows usable meat, and the pack still eats

## Rules

Read `design/handoffs/behaviour-ai-worker-rules-2026-10-12.md` first. It covers windows, the
5-minute limit, labd, builds, tests, knobs, saves, commits and the return format. Use graft
(`graft ask … --source`, `graft callers`) before grepping. Never wrap a `scripts/lab/lab`
command in `timeout`. Killing it mid-git leaves a stale `.git/index.lock` in the main
checkout; that happened twice today.

## Why

VH-TACTICS (`design/handoffs/vh-tactics-2026-10-12.md`) gave the Veilhound its own-senses
tactic (`crates/cubarium-voxel-fauna/src/tactics.rs`). Its rerun is in
`design/7_Research/vh-tactics-engage-2026-10-12.md` (job `1012-093723-vh-tactics-engage-9ee3`;
baseline in `design/7_Research/engage-baseline-2026-10-12.md`). Two results go against the
design:

1. **Lone calves escape.** A lone calf against 2–4 hounds is killed in 0/0/4 % of episodes
   (baseline 4/34/48 %), and the pack gives up on it in 96–100 %. Wrysk's ask, quoted in §0 of
   `design/behaviour-ai-2026-10-11.md`: "lone juveniles are the most defenseless". His topic-8
   target is a pack that kills mostly strayed calves.
2. **The choice never weighs the target.**
   - Every test commits to an adult, and the calf was chased in 0–1 of 50 family episodes.
   - Give-ups are stalls (14 of 20 sampled) and spent hounds (2), never a value break-off.
   - With today's placeholders, meat scales with mass (an adult is 3.5–3.9 meals, a calf
     0.6–0.85), risk is small, and `commit_value` is 0, so adults always win.

**The design's intent** (§4.3): `p_kill` rises with youth, lameness and isolation, and falls
with guards. Value is meat × `p_kill` − chase cost − risk. The test is where the choice gets
made. Read §0, §4 and §6 of the design.

## Do

Commit after each numbered step.

1. **Diagnose the lone-calf stall** in staged encounters (the harness, quick lane). Find out
   why hounds that commit to a lone calf don't catch it: the stall timer, the calf's speed or
   turning against the hound's, the strike's size window, the rush trigger, interception, or
   something else. Fix the mechanism, not a number, unless a number is plainly absurd; if it
   is, say why. Report the cause with evidence (event traces from a few seeds).
2. **Meat as usable meals.** A pack can't use more meat than its members can eat before the
   carcass is gone: their gut room now, plus what a carcass yields over the time they can
   return to it. Read how carcasses decay and how hounds feed, then make the value term count
   usable meals, not raw mass. This shapes the situation rather than scoring a behaviour
   (`WORKING_POLICY.md`, "Shape the situation").
   - With that, a hungry pack facing a guarded adult and an exposed calf should prefer the
     calf. A break-off should happen when the value goes below zero, not only on stalls.
   - Keep §4.3's form. Any new number is a knob.
3. **Rerun the encounter set** on the baseline seeds (quick lane), and report the table
   beside the baseline and the VH-TACTICS rerun. Add the `ends` field's reasons to the
   table.
4. **Live-world check: does the pack still eat?**
   - Run a labd A/B: main as of your branch point against your branch, on the wide world
     where the pack and a loftstrider family both found (see `config/` and
     `design/handoffs/*wide*`), 8 seeds × 1 sim-hour.
   - Read: pack members alive, pack energy, kills by prey lineage and the pack's diet share
     by energy, and each prey's numbers.
   - The desktop's slots are reserved for hours by another thread's A/B, so this job will
     queue. Submit it, hand back with the job id, and say what to read when it finishes.
   - EC2 is not yours to launch.
