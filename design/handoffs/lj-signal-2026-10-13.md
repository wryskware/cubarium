# LJ-SIGNAL: why 60 generations didn't move the lanternjaw brain

## Rules

Read `design/handoffs/behaviour-ai-worker-rules-2026-10-12.md` first (the 2026-10-12 rules hold).
- Never wrap `scripts/lab/lab` in `timeout`. Quick lane only for anything you run: `quick = true`,
  `hosts = ["desktop"]`, `cwd` set, under 15 minutes. A run that needs longer: write its spec,
  submit it, and hand back with the job id. Never wait on it.
- Build in your own worktree's `target/` (reflink-seeded) at -j 8 or lower. Job binaries go to
  the main checkout's `runs/bin/<name>-<commit>`.

## Why

The first stage-1 run (top layer only, bottom frozen at the clone) finished:
`runs/lab/1012-190203-brest-lj-stage1-589c/run/run/` (`generations.jsonl`, `checks.jsonl`,
`centres/`). Binary `runs/bin/cubarium-search-57bf03b5`, spec
`/tmp/claude-1000/-home-wrysk-wryskware-cubarium/7136f1a8-b5d9-47ee-9452-4b410a19291c/scratchpad/brest/lj-stage1.toml`
(16 worlds, 16 pairs, 10-minute windows, 60 generations, top-up lanternjaw=3:0.3, `per_body`
comparison, no kin file).

The check (brain beside its heuristic, 6 matched seeds × 600 s, deterministic) never moved:
brain-minus-heuristic energy gain per body was -0.047 at gen 0, -0.034, -0.040, -0.083,
-0.055, -0.026, and -0.035 at gen 60. The brain was ahead on 2/6 seeds throughout, 3/6 at the
end. The prediction was ≥4/6 by gen ~30. It patrols far more than its teacher (brain 34–64 %,
rules 6 %).

In the training log, per-body scores are noisy (sd ~1.4 against a mean of ~0.5–2), and the
training worlds drift. Lanternjaw bodies grow from 48 to 129, the mean score falls from 2.1 to
~0.5, and early generations place only 24 of 32 candidates. Gradient norm ~138, update RMS ~0.004.

The design (`design/behaviour-ai-2026-10-11.md` §10.3, §10.4, §11.7) expects this question:
- "a frozen-brain run measures the noise first";
- the comparison is a switch (`per_body`, `family`, `matched`);
- matched copies of a whole live window are "a cost choice, not new code";
- the first signal read decides whether to revise topic 10, and goes to Wrysk.

This is that read for the lanternjaw.

## Do

1. **Signal vs noise in this run, from its logs.** How much of a candidate's score is its
   variant, and how much is the world? Use the antithetic pairs, the placement holes (score 0
   for unplaced candidates), and drift across generations. Say whether the centre moved in a
   consistent direction at all.
2. **The frozen-brain noise level** (§10.3), if no run has measured it for the lanternjaw.
   Check `kin::matched::null_pair`, `NoiseReadout` and the KIN-TRAIN commits first. Run it
   small on the quick lane, or write the spec and hand back.
3. **What would carry a signal,** each with its cost in desktop-hours and its predicted effect.
   Candidates: matched live windows (`comparison = "matched"` for the lanternjaw); more pairs
   or bodies per candidate; refounding each generation instead of continuing drifting worlds;
   longer windows; the learning rate. Check each against the code: is it a switch, or new code?
4. **Fix a bug if you find one** (for example, a check or score that reads the wrong body),
   with a test. Anything else is a proposal.
5. **Hand back the next run's spec, ready to submit and not submitted,** for the option you
   recommend.
