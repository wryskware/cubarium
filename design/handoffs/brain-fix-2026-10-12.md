# BRAIN-FIX: fair competence checks, full labels, clones that can roost, a first training run

## Rules

Read `design/handoffs/behaviour-ai-worker-rules-2026-10-12.md` first. It covers windows, the
5-minute limit, labd, builds, tests, knobs, saves, commits and the return format. Use graft
before grepping.
- Never wrap `scripts/lab/lab` in `timeout`.
- Jobs under 15 min go on the quick lane (`quick = true`). Longer ones queue: submit them and
  hand back with the ids. EC2 is never yours to launch.

## Why

Read these first:
- `design/7_Research/brain-clones-2026-10-12.md`: the first per-mode clones, which are
  BRAIN-NEXT's output;
- §10 and §11.3 of `design/behaviour-ai-2026-10-11.md`;
- `design/handoffs/brain-next-2026-10-12.md` and the commit messages of merge 02c78cb3 and
  its parents, for the tools.

**Wrysk's bar:** a brain is worth using only if it beats its heuristic ("otherwise it wont be
worth using either").

**What the first clones showed:**
- The top layer picks the intent well.
- Movement competence is uneven:
  - the two-layer seedporter roosts in 0.19 of scenes, against 0.69 for plain and 0.94 for
    the rules;
  - rest and the lanternjaw's give-way fail for both clones.
- The rules themselves score low on groom (0.16), rest (0.16) and feed (0.13), so the scenes
  or their competence tests may not match what the heuristic actually does.
- The lanternjaw's teacher labels ambush, flee and rest as patrol, because `hunt::steer`
  answers every sample.

## Do

Commit after each numbered step.

1. **Fair competence checks.** For groom, rest and feed, find why the rules score low.
   Either the scene doesn't give the mode room to finish, or the test asks for something the
   heuristic doesn't do. Fix the scene or the test, with evidence (traces). Keep each test
   asking whether the mode achieves its purpose.
2. **Full lanternjaw labels.** Map the lanternjaw's real states to its menu: ambush (the wait
   at a lure), patrol, feed, give way, flee (any threat response it has), and rest. The
   labels must come from the heuristic's actual state, not a guess. If a state doesn't exist
   in the heuristic, say so rather than inventing one.
3. **Why the two-layer clone can't roost.**
   - Diagnose it: is it the climb's long action sequence, the hold timing, the subject (the
     crown) handling, too little data, or the same-sample readout?
   - Fix what's wrong in the brain or the cloning (data, updates, weighting by mode), not
     the scene.
   - Rerun both clones and report the per-mode table beside the first one.
4. **A first training run: lanternjaw, stage 1.**
   - The lanternjaw's menu won't change in the coming merges. The seedporter's and
     loftstrider's will (YOUNG-ROAM adds `explore`), so they wait.
   - Write the training spec: the cloned two-layer centre, stage 1 (top only), live windows
     with `per_body` comparison as today, plus the per-mode staged scenes as extra episodes if
     the trainer can take them cheaply.
   - Readouts every check: energy against the heuristic on matched seeds, per-mode
     competence, hold durations and switch rates.
   - Run a short smoke run (quick lane) to show it trains and writes back. Then submit the
     real run so it queues, and hand back with its id and what to read.
   - Predict, before it runs, what "beats the heuristic" should look like after N
     generations, and say how you'd know it's working.

## Boundaries

  `young-roam`) is waiting on a live check before it merges. Don't touch loftstrider or
  Veilhound behaviour, and stay off the seedporter's menu (YOUNG-ROAM changes it).

## Checks (written into tests as you go)

- Each fixed scene still triggers its mode in the heuristic, and the rules' competence there
  is now high.
- Lanternjaw labels: each state maps to its menu intent on a fixture.
- The roost fix: a test on the cause you found.
- The smoke training run's write-back loads and drives.

## Return

The rules file's format, plus:
- the before/after per-mode tables;
- the roost diagnosis with evidence;
- the training job id, what to read and your prediction;
- every departure, with why.
