# KIN-TRAIN (estimator): kin-weighted scores, the comparison switch, matched copies

## Rules

Read `design/handoffs/behaviour-ai-worker-rules-2026-10-12.md` first. It covers windows, the
5-minute limit, labd, builds, tests, knobs, saves, commits and the return format. Use graft
(`graft ask … --source`, `graft callers`) before grepping.

## Why

This is package 8 of `design/behaviour-ai-2026-10-11.md`, the trainer half. Wrysk's calls:
- topic 2: kin-weighted reward. Each body keeps its own variant, and its score adds `r` times
  each relative's own score.
- topic 10: matched copies on staged encounters, with per-body comparison kept for live
  windows and everyday skills. In his words: "i guess (a) but hard to say and we might want
  to keep the options open or revise once we start training".

Read §0 rows 2 and 10, §10.3, §10.4 and §11.7 in full.

The core problem (Astra review #1–3, §11.7 "The cancellation"):
- Today's scores are z-scored within each world and lineage (`pooled_scores`,
  `crates/cubarium-search/src/es/voxel/live/trainer.rs` ~136-161).
- With one pack per world, that cancels the shared kin term exactly.
- Antithetic twins are ring neighbours (`live/window.rs` ~228-245), so a mother and her calf
  often carry opposite signs of one perturbation.

## Do

Commit after each numbered step.

1. **Roster** (§11.7).
   - Fixed at the start of a window or episode, from true relations: `Animal::mother` for
     mother, calf and sibling; the pack record for packmates.
   - A newborn joins its mother's roster at birth.
   - Relatives' energy meters persist after death, so a killed calf's loss counts against
     its mother.
   - Each relative contributes its own base score, not its kin-augmented one.
   - One relation per pair, the closest: mother and calf first, then sibling, then packmate.
   - Heuristic relatives are metered too. Today `live/window.rs` ~670-672 drops bodies
     without a candidate.
   - Siblings are only those sharing a real recorded mother id. CONTRACTS (in parallel)
     records founders with a shared founding mother id that names no living animal; treat
     that the same way.
2. **Kin-weighted score.** Own base score
   `(E_end − E_start + E_given)/body_ref`, as today, plus `r_rel ×` each relative's own base
   score.
   - `r` is a serde knob per relation (calf, mother, sibling, packmate), 0.5 to start, per
     lineage. A lineage with every `r` at 0 scores exactly as today.
3. **The comparison switch**, per lineage, as serde config with three modes:
   - `per_body`: today's within-world z-scoring, the default for live windows. A lineage
     left at defaults trains exactly as today.
   - `family`: one shared variant per family or pack, compared across worlds (topic 2's
     fallback).
   - `matched`: the default for staged encounters. Two copies from one saved start; one
     focal animal carries `+ε` in one and `−ε` in the other, and everything else is
     identical. The focal animal and the sign rotate. The estimate is the difference in the
     focal's kin-weighted score.
4. **The matched-copies runner over any saved start state.** World, flora and fauna saves,
   plus brain state; the two-layer `BrainState` in
   `crates/cubarium-search/src/es/voxel/two_layer/brain.rs` is serde-ready.
   - Build it generically, so a staged encounter scene and a whole live window are the same
     machinery on different scenes (§11.7, "Kept open").
   - The ENGAGE package (Codex, branch `engage`) is building the staged encounter harness in
     parallel. Don't depend on its code. Define the small interface a scene must offer (found
     from seed or load a start, step, read scores), and say what ENGAGE must expose.
   - **Determinism decides whether matched copies work.** Run two copies with identical
     brains from one start and measure how far their scores diverge: the null-pair noise.
     The repo doesn't promise bit-identical runs (parallel schedules). If copies diverge,
     find out whether a deterministic mode exists for small scenes (one thread, a fixed
     schedule) and what it costs. Report the null-pair noise either way. This number
     decides whether topic 10's choice holds.
5. **Stage 1: perturb only the top** (§10.4).
   - For a lineage whose policy is a two-layer file, the variant builder perturbs only the
     top's range through `TwoLayerLayout::restrict(Layer::Top, …)` (`two_layer/shape.rs`
     ~369-395). Stage 2 perturbs both; the stage is a knob.
   - Plain lineages are unchanged.
6. **Readouts and checks** (§11.7 "Checks", §10.4 "Readouts"):
   - raw own, relative and combined scores per body;
   - missing signs are never scored as zero returns;
   - the frozen-brain noise check (no perturbation) and the signal check (repeated matched
     differences in a consistent direction, how often decisions change, held-out energy
     improvement, candidate coverage) as tooling that a run can switch on.

## Boundaries

- Don't edit `crates/cubarium-voxel-fauna/`: CONTRACTS is reworking it. Read what you need.
  If you truly need a fauna-side accessor, name it in the handback rather than adding it.

  as it is; small additive changes there are fine.
- No training runs beyond the short determinism and fixture checks. Real runs come after
  the adapter lands.

## Checks (written into tests as you go)

- **The algebraic check** (§11.7): a perturbation that improves only a relative's energy
  survives the whole scorer under `matched` and `family`. Under `per_body` with `r > 0` and
  one pack per world, show the cancellation, as documentation of why the switch exists.
- A lineage at defaults (`per_body`, `r` = 0) gives scores identical to today's on a fixture.
- Roster: a dead calf's loss counts against its mother; the closest relation wins; a founder
  with a phantom mother id has no live mother; heuristic relatives are metered.
- `matched`: the focal animal and sign rotate; non-focal bodies carry identical brains in
  both copies.
- Stage 1 leaves every bottom parameter of a two-layer variant untouched.
